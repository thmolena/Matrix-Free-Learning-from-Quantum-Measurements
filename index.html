Spectral-Frame CPTP-PINNs: Eigen-Operator Structure-Preserving Learning and Operator-System Spectral Truncation for Open Quantum Dynamics

Identification of Markovian open-quantum models from sparse, noisy data. The central contribution is a spectral-frame (interaction-picture eigen-operator) parameterization: the density network learns only the slow envelope ρ̃ = U†ρU in the Hamiltonian eigenbasis through the same hard Cholesky map, supplying the fast Bohr-frequency oscillations analytically and so removing spectral bias at the level of the representation. Band-limiting the interaction-picture generator to its slow Bohr eigen-operators is an operator-system spectral truncation, lifting the spectral-truncation construction of noncommutative geometry and C\*-algebraic kernel machines from static kernels to a completely positive dynamical generator. This resolves the principal open problem of the structure-preserving approach — reliable reconstruction of fast multi-level (qutrit leakage) dynamics.

This is a physics-informed neural network for open-quantum-system identification with the physics enforced in the architecture, not the loss: a hard Cholesky map keeps the learned state Hermitian, positive, and trace-one at every step (0 positivity violations), and a spectral-frame eigen-operator parameterization removes the spectral bias that defeats smooth networks on fast multi-level dynamics (0.985 mean qutrit-leakage fidelity vs ≤0.77 for plain/Fourier baselines). 1Background and Motivation

Open quantum systems are the working language of quantum hardware. Every superconducting, trapped-ion, photonic, spin, and neutral-atom processor extends beyond its ideal Hamiltonian into relaxation, dephasing, leakage, crosstalk, drift, and control-induced dissipation. Throughout this description, one object must stay physical at all times: the density matrix. For a finite Hilbert space ℋ ≅ ℂ^d, its admissible values form the state space

$$\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})=\{\rho\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}:\rho=\rho^\dagger,\ \rho\succeq 0,\ \operatorname{Tr}\rho=1\}.$$

For Markovian, time-local dynamics, the most general generator of a completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) semigroup is the Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan–Lindblad (GKSL) generator. It is the backbone of quantum noise modeling, control, and calibration.

Recovering such a generator from data is the inverse problem at the heart of device characterization, and existing approaches each pay a price. Classical process tomography reconstructs a channel but scales poorly with system size; Hamiltonian and Lindblad learning exploit dynamical structure yet still confront identifiability and optimization obstacles. Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) add flexible approximation and place the equation residual in the objective, yet a direct neural map t ↦ ρ_θ(t) is free to leave the manifold of Hermitian, positive semidefinite, unit-trace operators — the very properties that define a state.

The open problem. Penalizing negative eigenvalues differs from enforcing physicality during training: a penalty merely asks the optimizer to repair an already-invalid output. In parallel, dense Liouvillian implementations assemble the full d²×d² superoperator even when the model is fixed by a Hamiltonian and a handful of local jump operators. This work inverts both design choices: it renders invalid states unrepresentable, and lowers a symbolic open-system specification to kernels that bypass the dense superoperator entirely.

A second, orthogonal obstacle. Even with physicality made architectural, a direct map t ↦ ρ_θ(t) must still represent every fast coherent oscillation of the lab-frame trajectory, and a smooth-in-time network suffers spectral bias: it learns low frequencies first and resolves the rapid Bohr-frequency oscillations of multilevel systems only slowly and unreliably. The coherent generator ad_H = −i[H,·] is normal; in the Hamiltonian eigenbasis its eigen-operators are the transition operators |a⟩⟨b| with purely imaginary eigenvalues −iω_{ab}, ω_{ab}=E_a−E_b — precisely those fast oscillations. Moving to the interaction picture lets the network learn only the slow envelope while the fast oscillation is supplied analytically by the known U(t)=e^{−iHt}, removing spectral bias by a change of representation rather than a penalty.

The same decomposition truncates the kernel. In the interaction picture the residual generator is almost periodic; on a finite window it admits a Fourier series, and keeping the low-frequency band is a projection onto a finite operator system of slow eigen-operators. This is the spectral-truncation construction by which noncommutative geometry approximates a C*-algebra by finite-dimensional operator systems (Connes–van Suijlekom) and by which C*-algebraic kernel machines reinstate noncommutativity (Hashimoto et al.); here it is lifted from static kernels to a dynamical, completely positive generator, yielding a controlled multi-resolution hierarchy from the secular (Davies) generator through the rotating-wave approximation to the exact generator. The framework is presented in a compiler idiom: a model is written as an intermediate representation — Hilbert dimension, Hamiltonian terms, jump operators, rates, observables, batching, differentiation mode — and lowered to a dense Liouvillian, a structured residual kernel, or a spectrally truncated kernel, mirroring the high-level-intent-to-hardware-conscious-kernel philosophy of contemporary accelerator software and compatible with PyTorch, SciPy, QuTiP, and Qiskit.

2Mathematical Preliminaries

The formal objects the formulation depends on, stated once and reused throughout.

Markovian generator (GKSL form)

A time-independent Markovian master equation reads

$$\frac{d\rho}{dt}=\mathcal{L}_\Theta[\rho]=-i[H_\Theta,\rho]+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_k\!\left(L_k\rho L_k^\dagger-\tfrac12\{L_k^\dagger L_k,\rho\}\right),\qquad H_\Theta=H_\Theta^\dagger,\ \gamma_k\ge 0.$$

Using the column-major identity vec(AρB)=(Bᵀ⊗A)vec(ρ), the dense Liouvillian is

$$\mathbb{L}_\Theta=-i\,(I\otimes H_\Theta-H_\Theta^{T}\otimes I)+\sum_k\gamma_k\!\left(L_k^{*}\otimes L_k-\tfrac12 I\otimes L_k^\dagger L_k-\tfrac12(L_k^\dagger L_k)^{T}\otimes I\right),$$

This form stores d⁴ complex scalars; evaluating the generator directly instead stores only H and the L_k, i.e. O(md²) — the gap the compiler exploits.

Hard-constrained density network (the Cholesky map)

Let B_φ:[0,T]→ℂ^{d×d} be a neural network whose output is interpreted as a lower-triangular factor A_φ(t) with strictly positive diagonal,

$$A_\phi(t)_{ij}=0\ (ij),$$

and define the state through the differentiable, architecture-level map that is physical by construction,

$$\rho_\phi(t)=\frac{A_\phi(t)A_\phi(t)^\dagger}{\operatorname{Tr}\!\big[A_\phi(t)A_\phi(t)^\dagger\big]}.$$

The map carries d² real degrees of freedom against the d²−1 of a full-rank trace-normalized state, so its redundancy is mild, and rank-deficient (pure) states are recovered as limits.

Generator parameterization (GKSL by construction)

Two parameterizations keep the generator in GKSL form. When the jump operators are known but the rates are not, γ_k(ξ_k)=softplus(ξ_k)+γ_min with γ_min ≥ 0; for a full Kossakowski matrix in a fixed basis {F_a}, the positive semidefinite factorization C_η=B_η B_η^† ⪰ 0 gives

$$\mathcal{L}_\Theta[\rho]=-i[H_\Theta,\rho]+\sum_{a,b=1}^{q}(C_\eta)_{ab}\!\left(F_a\rho F_b^\dagger-\tfrac12\{F_b^\dagger F_a,\rho\}\right),\qquad H_\theta(t)=\sum_\ell\theta_\ell(t)\,G_\ell.$$

Physics-informed objective

Training minimizes three terms: a masked data fit, an initial-condition match, and the Lindblad residual evaluated at collocation points {τ_ℓ}:

$$\mathcal{J}(\phi,\Theta)=\lambda_{\mathrm{data}}\,\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{data}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{phys}}\,\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{phys}}+\lambda_0\,\mathcal{J}_0,\qquad \mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{phys}}=\frac{1}{N_c}\sum_\ell\big\|\dot\rho_\phi(\tau_\ell)-\mathcal{L}_\Theta[\rho_\phi(\tau_\ell)]\big\|_F^2,$$

with J_data = (Σ m_ij)⁻¹ Σ m_ij (Tr[O_j ρ_φ(t_i)] − y_ij)² and J_0 = ‖ρ_φ(0) − ρ_0‖_F². The time derivative ρ̇_φ comes from automatic differentiation of the density network; the residual term L_Θ[ρ_φ] is supplied by the compiler in either its dense or structured mode.

Spectral frame and operator-system truncation

Diagonalize the frame Hamiltonian H=Σ_a E_a |a⟩⟨a|; with U(t)=e^{−iHt} the network learns the slow interaction-picture envelope and rebuilds the lab state analytically,

$$\rho_\phi(t)=U(t)\,\widetilde\rho_\phi(t)\,U(t)^\dagger,\qquad (\widetilde\rho_\phi)_{ab}\mapsto e^{-i\omega_{ab}t}(\widetilde\rho_\phi)_{ab},\quad \omega_{ab}=E_a-E_b,$$

where ρ̃_φ is produced by the same Cholesky map (so it is a valid density matrix) and the physics residual is evaluated in the interaction picture with rotated jumps L̃_k(t)=U(t)†L_kU(t). In this frame the generator is almost periodic, 𝓛̃_t = Σ_k e^{ikω₀t}𝓛_k, ω₀=2π/T; the level-N operator-system spectral truncation keeps the band |k|≤N, with N=0 the secular (Davies) generator, N=1 the rotating-wave approximation, and the full band the exact generator, certified by the out-of-band weight η_N = Σ_{|k|>N}‖𝓛_k‖.

3Problem Formulation

Fix a Hilbert dimension d, an initial state ρ_0 ∈ 𝒟(ℋ), and observables {O_j}_{j=1}^{p}. The data are noisy, possibly incomplete measurements

$$y_{ij}\approx \operatorname{Tr}\!\big[O_j\,\rho(t_i)\big]+\varepsilon_{ij},\qquad m_{ij}\in\{0,1\}\ \text{(observation mask)},$$

sampled from an unknown trajectory generated by an unknown GKSL generator 𝓛_{Θ*}. The task is to recover two things jointly: (i) a trajectory ρ_φ:[0,T]→𝒟(ℋ) consistent with the data, and (ii) the generator parameters Θ — the Hamiltonian coefficients and dissipative rates. The recovery is constrained throughout: every estimate must be a legal density matrix at every time, every iteration, and every evaluation point, and the recovered generator must be a legal Lindbladian. The problem is generally ill-posed, since recovery of a parameter direction requires that the chosen observables excite it — precisely what the identifiability analysis below quantifies.

4Principal Contributions

  1. A hard-constrained density network. The Cholesky density map enforces Hermiticity, positive semidefiniteness, and unit trace exactly — not approximately — at every time point, every training iteration, and every evaluation point.
  2. A spectral-frame (eigen-operator) parameterization. The network learns only the slow interaction-picture envelope ρ̃(t)=U(t)†ρ(t)U(t) in the Hamiltonian eigenbasis through the same hard map, supplying the fast Bohr-frequency oscillations analytically via the known U(t)=exp(−iHt). Spectral bias is removed at the architecture level, and the well-conditioned envelope derivative makes the dissipative rates identifiable by a linear least-squares readout.
  3. Operator-system spectral truncation of the generator. Band-limiting the interaction-picture generator to its slow Bohr eigen-operators is a projection onto a finite operator system, giving a controlled multi-resolution hierarchy — secular (Davies) and rotating-wave as its coarsest levels, converging to the exact generator — with an a posteriori out-of-band-weight error certificate. This lifts the spectral-truncation construction of noncommutative geometry (Connes–van Suijlekom) and C\*-algebraic kernel machines (Hashimoto et al.) from static kernels to a completely positive dynamical generator.
  4. A CPTP-by-construction generator parameterization and matrix-free compiler. Softplus-positive rates and positive semidefinite Kossakowski factorizations C=BB^† keep the generator in GKSL form; a symbolic open-system intermediate representation lowers to dense, structured, or spectrally truncated kernels that agree to floating-point precision.
  5. Eight exact mathematical guarantees and a fully reproducible study. Including spectral-frame physicality-and-equivalence and the operator-system truncation bound; every figure and number is emitted by the ctpcpinn package over six experiments, five seeds each, mean ± 95% confidence interval.

The claims are deliberately bounded: the experiments establish the value of the structural constraints and the compiler path on controlled simulations. Their scope stops short of quantum-hardware validation, universal superiority over every baseline, and production-scale GPU performance.

5Methodological Novelty

The novelty is best read against named prior art, one comparison at a time:

  • Versus spectral-truncation kernel machines (Hashimoto et al.; Connes–van Suijlekom operator systems). Spectral truncation approximates a C*-algebra by finite-dimensional operator systems, and has recently been used to reinstate noncommutativity in static kernel machines. This work lifts the same construction from static kernels to the learning and approximation of a completely positive dynamical generator: band-limiting the interaction-picture generator to its slow Bohr eigen-operators yields a multi-resolution hierarchy — with the secular (Davies) and rotating-wave generators as its coarsest levels — equipped with a computable truncation-error certificate.
  • Versus spectral-bias remedies (Fourier features; Tancik et al.). Rather than encode the input to help a network learn high frequencies, the spectral frame carries the fast, exactly-known Bohr oscillation analytically and lets the network represent only the slow envelope — a change of representation, not of loss or encoding, that inherits the physicality guarantee.
  • Versus completely-positive projection methods (Knee et al.). Standard pipelines repair an invalid estimate after the fact by projecting onto the positive cone. Here invalid states fall outside the model's expressive range: the Cholesky map lands in 𝒟(ℋ) by construction, eliminating the projection step entirely.
  • Versus Lindblad / Hamiltonian learning (Boulant et al.; Wang et al.). The framework retains structured identification while replacing unconstrained trajectory estimation with a hard density parameterization, so optimizer capacity stays inside the physical set throughout training.
  • Versus inverse PINNs (Raissi, Karniadakis; Wang et al.). The residual objective is shared, while the hypothesis class is narrowed to physically valid states and CPTP generators — the same structure-preserving principle behind Hamiltonian, Lagrangian, and symplectic networks, which constrain the model to improve learning.
  • Versus neural quantum states / neural tomography (Carleo & Troyer; Torlai et al.). Those parameterize wavefunctions or measurement distributions of states; this work parameterizes the generator of open-system dynamics, with a CPTP guarantee built in.
  • The "compiler" framing. A model is written as an intermediate representation — Hilbert dimension, Hamiltonian terms, jump operators, rates, observables, batching, differentiation mode — and lowered to dense, structured, sparse, or tensor-local kernels, aligning open-system identification with the design philosophy of modern accelerator and quantum software stacks.

6Theoretical Guarantees

Eight exact results — not empirical trends — transcribed verbatim from the manuscript, each followed by a one-line reading of what it buys.

Theorem 1 — Exact density-matrix physicality. Let A:[0,T]→ℂ^{d×d} be continuous with A(t)≠0 for all t, and ρ_A(t)=A(t)A(t)^† / Tr(A(t)A(t)^†). Then ρ_A(t)∈𝒟(ℋ) for every t. If A is differentiable, ρ_A is differentiable, Hermitian, and Tr ρ̇_A(t)=0.
Every training step emits a legal state short of a floating-point failure — the mechanism behind the measured zero positivity-violation rate.
Theorem 2 — Universal approximation of continuous density trajectories. For any continuous ρ:[0,T]→𝒟(ℋ) and full-rank σ, and every ε>0, there exists a finite network A_φ with nonzero output such that sup_t ‖A_φA_φ^†/Tr(A_φA_φ^†) − ρ(t)‖_F < ε.
The hard constraint costs no expressive power: every continuous physical trajectory remains approximable inside the constrained hypothesis class.
Theorem 3 — GKSL preservation. With H_θ=H_θ^† and γ_k(ξ_k)=softplus(ξ_k)+γ_min, γ_min≥0, the generator is a GKSL generator and e^{t𝓛_Θ} is CPTP for all t≥0. The Kossakowski parameterization with C=BB^† is likewise a GKSL generator.
Because the learned generator is always a legal Lindbladian, it can be propagated to held-out initial states with an exact solver.
Theorem 4 — A posteriori residual certificate. Let ρ solve ρ̇=𝓛[ρ], ρ(0)=ρ_0 with 𝓛 generating a CPTP semigroup, and let ρ̂ be a differentiable Hermitian trace-one path with residual r(t)=ρ̂̇(t)−𝓛[ρ̂(t)]. Then $$\tfrac12\|\hat\rho(t)-\rho(t)\|_1\le\tfrac12\|\hat\rho(0)-\rho_0\|_1+\tfrac12\int_0^t\|r(s)\|_1\,ds.$$
The bound depends only on the learned path, so a small training residual is itself a computable guarantee on the trace distance to the true dynamics, computed without a reference trajectory.
Theorem 5 — Local identifiability. For a smooth GKSL generator with observation Jacobian J(Θ*) of full column rank and smallest singular value s_min>0, any estimate with ‖f(Θ̂)−f(Θ*)‖₂≤δ obeys ‖Θ̂−Θ*‖₂ ≤ 2δ/s_min on a suitable neighborhood.
Hard constraints cannot rescue uninformative observables: a near-singular sensitivity Gramian degrades the recovery bound — exactly the regime in which the qutrit rates are hard to pin down.
Theorem 6 — Dense versus structured residual complexity. For a d-dimensional system with m dense Lindblad operators: dense Liouvillian storage is O(d⁴), construction O(md⁴), and O(d⁴) per dense matvec; structured evaluation costs O(md³) arithmetic and O(md²) storage. Over N collocation states, dense is O(md⁴+Nd⁴) and structured O(Nmd³).
The per-evaluation ratio is Θ(d/m) and the storage ratio Θ(d²/m), so the structured route wins by a widening margin in both arithmetic and memory as d grows.
Theorem 7 — Spectral-frame physicality and equivalence. For a Hermitian frame Hamiltonian H with U(t)=e^{−iHt} and any envelope ρ̃:[0,T]→𝒟(ℋ), the lab state ρ(t)=U(t)ρ̃(t)U(t)†∈𝒟(ℋ) for every t; and ρ solves the lab master equation iff ρ̃ solves the interaction-picture equation ρ̃̇=𝓛̃_t[ρ̃], whose generator is GKSL for each t with rotated jumps L̃_k(t)=U(t)†L_kU(t).
The spectral frame is exactly physical and exactly equivalent to the lab dynamics, so the network may learn the slow envelope — free of spectral bias — without sacrificing the state-space guarantee.
Theorem 8 — Operator-system spectral-truncation certificate. The interaction-picture generator has the Fourier series 𝓛̃_t=Σ_k e^{ikω₀t}𝓛_k. Its level-N truncation 𝓛̃_t^{(N)}=Σ_{|k|≤N} e^{ikω₀t}𝓛_k satisfies sup_t‖𝓛̃_t−𝓛̃_t^{(N)}‖ ≤ Σ_{|k|>N}‖𝓛_k‖ = η_N → 0, is Hermiticity- and trace-preserving, and (with Theorem 4) bounds the trajectory error by ½∫η_N‖ρ̃(s)‖₁ds, vanishing at full band.
A single dial trades cost for fidelity — secular and rotating-wave generators at the coarse end, the exact generator (and the structured kernel) at the full band — with an a posteriori certificate at every setting.

Two auxiliary results round out the theory: a Corollary extending physicality to low-rank-plus-isotropic variants, and a Proposition giving a Frobenius-norm analogue of the certificate via Grönwall's inequality.

9Significance and Impact

The two central claims are that physicality belongs in the architecture as an invariant rather than in the loss as an afterthought, and that the fast coherent structure of open-system dynamics should be carried by an analytic change of frame rather than learned — so the network represents only what is genuinely unknown. The mathematics shows these choices cost nothing in expressivity (Theorem 2), keep the learned generator CPTP (Theorem 3), remain exactly physical and exactly equivalent to the lab dynamics (Theorem 7), supply a posteriori certificates from both the residual (Theorem 4) and the spectral-truncation tail (Theorem 8), and admit structured compilation that sidesteps dense Liouvillian storage (Theorem 6). The spectral frame resolves the principal open problem of the prior approach — turning an unreliable fast-reconstruction (mean fidelity ≈0.5–0.8, intervals as wide as the means) into a reliable one (≈0.98 ± 0.01) — and the operator-system truncation furnishes a single dial between the secular generator and the exact one. Device characterization ultimately requires a transferable open-system model rather than a fitted curve — a generator that propagates to held-out preparations at ≈0.97 fidelity.

Limitations, stated in full.
  • Rate sloppiness under measurement noise. With the trajectory now reliably reconstructed, the dominant decay rates are recovered, but a weakly excited rate (the qutrit dephasing γ_φ) is a sloppy direction at 2% noise — exactly as the local identifiability bound predicts (Theorem 5), since the readout is exact on the noiseless envelope. Richer measurement designs (active experiment design) and derivative denoising are the natural next steps.
  • Known frame Hamiltonian. The spectral frame and the operator-system truncation use the eigenbasis of a known coherent part; when it is unknown or strongly time-dependent the frame must be co-learned or chosen adaptively (a partial static frame already removes the dominant oscillations).
  • Simulation scope. The experiments are synthetic CPU simulations; they validate the implementation and the theory, leaving device performance to future hardware studies.
  • Markovian assumption. The framework presumes GKSL dynamics; non-Markovian systems require extended state spaces, memory kernels, or process tensors.
  • Scale. This is a small, fully reproducible proof of concept (five seeds per training experiment), distinct from a state-of-the-art benchmark sweep.

The clearest engineering path forward is a compiler that ingests a high-level noise model and emits fused, batched, spectrally truncated, differentiable kernels for accelerator execution. The scientific path forward follows from the limitations above: co-learned or adaptive frames, richer measurement designs, non-Markovian embeddings, and hardware-calibration datasets.

10Reproducibility

Every number in the manuscript is written directly to its figure and table file by the ctpcpinn package (submission/code), with none entered by hand. The artifact is a pip-installable package, ctpcpinn (version 2.0.0, MIT, Python ≥ 3.10), whose ctpcpinn/spectral.py module implements the spectral frame and the operator-system truncation.

# 1. Obtain the source tree and install the package
cd submission/code
pip install .                 # runtime deps: numpy, scipy, torch, matplotlib, cycler

# 2. Regenerate every committed table and figure (six experiments)
python run_full.py            # or the console entry point:
ctpcpinn-reproduce
ctpcpinn-validate             # invariant checks, incl. the spectral frame

# 3. For bit-identical numbers, pin the exact environment
pip install ".[exact]"        # numpy 2.4.2, scipy 1.17.1, torch 2.10.0, matplotlib 3.10.8

Each training experiment runs over seeds {0,1,2,3,4} and reports mean ± 95% Student-t CI; the compiler benchmark reports the median over repeated timings. Configuration: 3000 Adam epochs at learning rate 10⁻³, 100 time points, noise standard deviation 0.02. The ctpcpinn/foundations.py module gives a from-scratch theory-to-code map, and run_one.py reproduces a single experiment. All simulations run on CPU; they are deliberately modest in scale and stand as a controlled proof of concept, separate from hardware data or state-of-the-art claims.

The contribution sits at the meeting point of several lines of work. It follows the structured-identification philosophy of Lindblad and Hamiltonian learning (Boulant et al. [11]; Wang et al. [12]) but replaces unconstrained trajectory estimation with a hard density-matrix map, in contrast to completely-positive projection methods that repair an estimate after the fact (Knee et al. [10]). It is closest to inverse physics-informed neural networks (Raissi et al. [13]; Karniadakis et al. [14]) and to structure-preserving scientific networks (Greydanus et al. [20]). Its operator-system spectral truncation lifts the noncommutative-geometry construction of Connes and van Suijlekom [39] and the C*-algebraic kernel machines of Hashimoto et al. [41] from static kernels to a completely positive dynamical generator, complementing the deterministic Fourier-feature remedy for spectral bias of Tancik et al. [43].
