CPTP-Compiler-PINNs: structure-preserving learning for open quantum dynamics.

A physics-informed framework for identifying Markovian (GKSL) open-system models from sparse, noisy observations, in which physicality is an architectural invariant rather than an after-the-fact penalty: a Cholesky density network that is Hermitian, PSD and unit-trace by construction; a softplus / positive-Kossakowski generator that stays completely-positive and trace-preserving; and a matrix-free compiler that lowers an open-system IR to dense or structured Lindblad residual kernels.

Reported conservatively. On full single-qubit data a classical least-squares fit that already knows the exact model is the most accurate method — we keep that as the transparent reference. The structural constraint pays off where it is designed to: it guarantees physicality at every step (zero positivity violations) and degrades gracefully under sparse measurements, where it sits one-to-two orders of magnitude below the soft and unconstrained baselines. Background & Motivation

The inverse problem for open quantum systems, and the structure-preservation gap

A real superconducting, trapped-ion, photonic, spin, or neutral-atom processor is never described by its ideal Hamiltonian alone — it also carries relaxation, dephasing, leakage, crosstalk, drift, and control-induced dissipation. The object that must stay physical throughout is the density matrix, which for lives in

When the dynamics are Markovian and time-local, the generator of the completely-positive trace-preserving (CPTP) semigroup has the Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan–Lindblad (GKSL) form. The inverse problem — inferring a physically admissible generator from incomplete, noisy observations — underlies device characterization, noise modeling, and control. Process tomography reconstructs a channel but scales poorly; Hamiltonian and Lindblad learning exploit structure but face identifiability and optimization challenges.

Physics-informed neural networks add equation residuals to the training objective, but a direct map is not guaranteed to stay Hermitian, PSD, or unit-trace. A penalty for negative eigenvalues is not equivalent to physicality during training — it merely asks the optimizer to repair an invalid output after the fact. On the Bloch ball a qubit state is valid iff , yet an unconstrained can leave the ball during optimization, corrupting residuals and parameter estimates. This framework takes the opposite design principle: make invalid states unrepresentable.

Tutorial & Background

How to read the CPTP-PINN contribution

The problem is inverse modelling: infer an open quantum system's generator from partial observations while keeping every learned state physically valid. A density matrix must be Hermitian, positive semidefinite, and trace one. A valid Markovian generator must be in GKSL/Lindblad form so it produces completely positive, trace-preserving dynamics. This project builds those requirements into the model instead of treating them as optional penalties.

Input

Sparse, noisy time-series observations of open-system trajectories, including qubit, two-qubit, and qutrit settings used as reproducible validation problems.

Mathematical core

The density map \(\rho=A A^\dagger/\mathrm{Tr}(A A^\dagger)\) makes invalid states unrepresentable, while softplus rates and PSD Kossakowski matrices keep the generator CPTP.

Statistical result

The hard-constrained model reports 0 positivity violations, sparse-data trace distance 0.004-0.010, two-qubit held-out fidelity 0.97 +/- 0.005, and 5.9-12.9x matrix-free compiler speedup.

Hiring-manager signal: this is not merely a neural-network fit. It combines mathematical invariants, inverse-problem design, compiler implementation, reproducible experiments, explicit limitations, and an interactive notebook viewer.
Contributions & Novelty

What is new

The contribution is a mathematically constrained, accelerator-aware formulation for open-system identification — not hardware data and not a state-of-the-art claim. Three constructions are new, supported by six exact (non-statistical) guarantees and a reproducible, statistically reported study.

  1. A hard-constrained density network.

    A Cholesky density map enforces Hermiticity, positive semidefiniteness, and unit trace exactly — at every time point, every training iteration, and every evaluation point. Physicality is architectural, not penalized.

  2. A CPTP generator parameterization.

    Nonnegative Lindblad rates via softplus (), and full Kossakowski matrices as PSD Gram matrices (). The learned generator remains in GKSL form, so is CPTP for all .

  3. A matrix-free open-system compiler.

    A symbolic IR (Hilbert dimension, Hamiltonian terms, jump operators, rates, observables, batching, differentiation mode) is lowered to either a dense Liouvillian or a structured residual kernel that stores only and the jump operators. The two agree to floating-point precision; a complexity theorem quantifies the gap.

  4. Six mathematical guarantees.

    Exact physicality of the density map; universal approximation of continuous density trajectories; GKSL/CPTP preservation of the learned generator; an a-posteriori trace-norm residual-to-error certificate; a local identifiability bound from the sensitivity Gramian; and a dense-vs-structured compiler complexity theorem.

  5. A reproducible, statistically reported study with baselines.

    Five simulation studies, each repeated over five seeds and reported as mean ± 95% CI, against a soft-positivity-penalty PINN, an unconstrained network, and a classical model-based least-squares fit.

Experimental Design & Statistics

Five reproducible studies, five seeds, 95% confidence intervals

All numerical values are produced by the scripts in submission/code and written directly to the table and figure files — none are entered by hand. Simulations run on CPU (PyTorch + SciPy), with Adam for the physics-informed runs.

Seeds & intervals

Each training experiment is repeated over seeds {0,1,2,3,4}; every seed redraws both the network initialization and the measurement-noise realization. We report the mean ± a 95% Student- confidence interval over the five seeds. The compiler benchmark reports the median over 50 repeated timings.

Invariant checks

Hermiticity, PSD, and unit trace are verified at every output (positivity-violation rate is reported as a column). The compiler's dense and structured paths are checked to agree to floating-point precision, at machine precision.

Baselines & ablations

Four methods where applicable: the hard-constrained CPTP-PINN; a soft-penalty PINN (eigenvalue-negativity penalty, ); an unconstrained network; and a classical least-squares fit using the exact model. The sparse-measurement ablation masks a random fraction (1.0, 0.5, 0.25, 0.10) of observation entries.

Transparent framing: on full single-qubit data, a classical least-squares fit that already knows the exact model structure is the strongest method — recovering the dissipative rates to ≈2%. The CPTP-PINN's value is not beating that fit on dense data; it is guaranteed physicality and graceful degradation under sparse measurements. The qutrit result is reported as the principal open problem, not a success.

Conclusion & Impact

Physicality as an architectural invariant — meaning, limits, outlook

The conceptual point is that physicality should be enforced by the architecture rather than requested by a penalty, and the mathematics shows this invariant costs nothing essential: universal approximation still holds, the learned generator stays CPTP, residuals furnish an a-posteriori trace-norm certificate, and structured compilation avoids materializing a dense Liouvillian. The experiments are deliberately modest but authentic and statistically reported.

What the hard constraint confers

It removes the competition between data fit, residual fit, and a positivity penalty. The payoff is clearest in two places the experiments isolate: a physical trajectory at every step (zero violations), and graceful degradation under sparse measurements. In larger systems, where the positive cone is a vanishingly small subset of Hermitian trace-one matrices, the gap is expected to widen.

What the compiler confers

Whether the residual can be evaluated without forming an object of size . Structured kernels still face exponential Hilbert-space growth but avoid an unnecessary square of the density-vector dimension — the difference between a toy prototype and a path that can exploit locality, batching, sparsity, and GPU memory hierarchy.

Transparent limitations

Fast multi-level (qutrit) reconstruction is the principal open problem — not reliable across seeds. The study uses synthetic CPU simulations (hardware data is left for future work). The framework assumes Markovian/GKSL dynamics. Identifiability is measurement-dependent. The study is a small proof of concept (five seeds), not a state-of-the-art claim.

Outlook: larger multi-qubit systems, hardware-calibration datasets, non-Markovian embeddings (extended state spaces, memory kernels, process tensors), interaction-picture residuals and derivative denoising for the fast-dynamics regime, and a compiler that takes a high-level noise model and emits fused, batched, differentiable accelerator kernels.
