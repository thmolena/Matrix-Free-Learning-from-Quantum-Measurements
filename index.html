CPTP-Compiler-PINNs: Structure-Preserving Residual Learning and Matrix-Free Compilation for Open Quantum Dynamics

Identification of Markovian open-quantum models from sparse, noisy data, founded on two design principles: physicality is an invariant of the architecture rather than a penalty applied after the fact, and the Lindblad residual is evaluated by a compiler that bypasses the dense d²×d² Liouvillian. The hard constraint is proved free of expressive cost and yields a transferable, certified generator.

1Background and Motivation

Open quantum systems are the working language of quantum hardware. Every superconducting, trapped-ion, photonic, spin, and neutral-atom processor extends beyond its ideal Hamiltonian into relaxation, dephasing, leakage, crosstalk, drift, and control-induced dissipation. Throughout this description, one object must stay physical at all times: the density matrix. For a finite Hilbert space ℋ ≅ ℂ^d, its admissible values form the state space

$$\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})=\{\rho\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}:\rho=\rho^\dagger,\ \rho\succeq 0,\ \operatorname{Tr}\rho=1\}.$$

For Markovian, time-local dynamics, the most general generator of a completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) semigroup is the Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan–Lindblad (GKSL) generator. It is the backbone of quantum noise modeling, control, and calibration.

Recovering such a generator from data is the inverse problem at the heart of device characterization, and existing approaches each pay a price. Classical process tomography reconstructs a channel but scales poorly with system size; Hamiltonian and Lindblad learning exploit dynamical structure yet still confront identifiability and optimization obstacles. Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) add flexible approximation and place the equation residual in the objective, yet a direct neural map t ↦ ρ_θ(t) is free to leave the manifold of Hermitian, positive semidefinite, unit-trace operators — the very properties that define a state.

The open problem. Penalizing negative eigenvalues differs from enforcing physicality during training: a penalty merely asks the optimizer to repair an already-invalid output. In parallel, dense Liouvillian implementations assemble the full d²×d² superoperator even when the model is fixed by a Hamiltonian and a handful of local jump operators. This work inverts both design choices: it renders invalid states unrepresentable, and lowers a symbolic open-system specification to kernels that bypass the dense superoperator entirely.

2Mathematical Preliminaries

The formal objects the formulation depends on, stated once and reused throughout.

Markovian generator (GKSL form)

A time-independent Markovian master equation reads

$$\frac{d\rho}{dt}=\mathcal{L}_\Theta[\rho]=-i[H_\Theta,\rho]+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_k\!\left(L_k\rho L_k^\dagger-\tfrac12\{L_k^\dagger L_k,\rho\}\right),\qquad H_\Theta=H_\Theta^\dagger,\ \gamma_k\ge 0.$$

Using the column-major identity vec(AρB)=(Bᵀ⊗A)vec(ρ), the dense Liouvillian is

$$\mathbb{L}_\Theta=-i\,(I\otimes H_\Theta-H_\Theta^{T}\otimes I)+\sum_k\gamma_k\!\left(L_k^{*}\otimes L_k-\tfrac12 I\otimes L_k^\dagger L_k-\tfrac12(L_k^\dagger L_k)^{T}\otimes I\right),$$

This form stores d⁴ complex scalars; evaluating the generator directly instead stores only H and the L_k, i.e. O(md²) — the gap the compiler exploits.

Hard-constrained density network (the Cholesky map)

Let B_φ:[0,T]→ℂ^{d×d} be a neural network whose output is interpreted as a lower-triangular factor A_φ(t) with strictly positive diagonal,

$$A_\phi(t)_{ij}=0\ (ij),$$

and define the state through the differentiable, architecture-level map that is physical by construction,

$$\rho_\phi(t)=\frac{A_\phi(t)A_\phi(t)^\dagger}{\operatorname{Tr}\!\big[A_\phi(t)A_\phi(t)^\dagger\big]}.$$

The map carries d² real degrees of freedom against the d²−1 of a full-rank trace-normalized state, so its redundancy is mild, and rank-deficient (pure) states are recovered as limits.

Generator parameterization (GKSL by construction)

Two parameterizations keep the generator in GKSL form. When the jump operators are known but the rates are not, γ_k(ξ_k)=softplus(ξ_k)+γ_min with γ_min ≥ 0; for a full Kossakowski matrix in a fixed basis {F_a}, the positive semidefinite factorization C_η=B_η B_η^† ⪰ 0 gives

$$\mathcal{L}_\Theta[\rho]=-i[H_\Theta,\rho]+\sum_{a,b=1}^{q}(C_\eta)_{ab}\!\left(F_a\rho F_b^\dagger-\tfrac12\{F_b^\dagger F_a,\rho\}\right),\qquad H_\theta(t)=\sum_\ell\theta_\ell(t)\,G_\ell.$$

Physics-informed objective

Training minimizes three terms: a masked data fit, an initial-condition match, and the Lindblad residual evaluated at collocation points {τ_ℓ}:

$$\mathcal{J}(\phi,\Theta)=\lambda_{\mathrm{data}}\,\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{data}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{phys}}\,\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{phys}}+\lambda_0\,\mathcal{J}_0,\qquad \mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{phys}}=\frac{1}{N_c}\sum_\ell\big\|\dot\rho_\phi(\tau_\ell)-\mathcal{L}_\Theta[\rho_\phi(\tau_\ell)]\big\|_F^2,$$

with J_data = (Σ m_ij)⁻¹ Σ m_ij (Tr[O_j ρ_φ(t_i)] − y_ij)² and J_0 = ‖ρ_φ(0) − ρ_0‖_F². The time derivative ρ̇_φ comes from automatic differentiation of the density network; the residual term L_Θ[ρ_φ] is supplied by the compiler in either its dense or structured mode.

3Problem Formulation

Fix a Hilbert dimension d, an initial state ρ_0 ∈ 𝒟(ℋ), and observables {O_j}_{j=1}^{p}. The data are noisy, possibly incomplete measurements

$$y_{ij}\approx \operatorname{Tr}\!\big[O_j\,\rho(t_i)\big]+\varepsilon_{ij},\qquad m_{ij}\in\{0,1\}\ \text{(observation mask)},$$

sampled from an unknown trajectory generated by an unknown GKSL generator 𝓛_{Θ*}. The task is to recover two things jointly: (i) a trajectory ρ_φ:[0,T]→𝒟(ℋ) consistent with the data, and (ii) the generator parameters Θ — the Hamiltonian coefficients and dissipative rates. The recovery is constrained throughout: every estimate must be a legal density matrix at every time, every iteration, and every evaluation point, and the recovered generator must be a legal Lindbladian. The problem is generally ill-posed, since recovery of a parameter direction requires that the chosen observables excite it — precisely what the identifiability analysis below quantifies.

4Principal Contributions

  1. A hard-constrained density network. The Cholesky density map enforces Hermiticity, positive semidefiniteness, and unit trace exactly — not approximately — at every time point, every training iteration, and every evaluation point.
  2. A CPTP-by-construction generator parameterization. Nonnegative Lindblad rates pass through softplus, and full Kossakowski matrices are factored as positive semidefinite Gram matrices C=BB^†, so the learned generator can never leave GKSL form.
  3. A matrix-free open-system compiler. A symbolic open-system intermediate representation is lowered to either a dense superoperator or a structured residual kernel; a dispatch policy picks the mode by dimension and batch size, and the two modes agree to floating-point precision.
  4. Six exact mathematical guarantees. Exact physicality, universal trajectory approximation, CPTP semigroup preservation, an a posteriori trace-norm residual certificate, a local identifiability bound, and a dense-versus-structured complexity separation — each proved in full.
  5. A fully reproducible study with statistics and baselines. Every figure and number is emitted by the accompanying scripts; each training experiment is repeated over five seeds and reported as mean ± 95% confidence interval, against a soft positivity penalty, an unconstrained network, and a classical model-based least-squares fit.

The claims are deliberately bounded: the experiments establish the value of the structural constraints and the compiler path on controlled simulations. Their scope stops short of quantum-hardware validation, universal superiority over every baseline, and production-scale GPU performance.

5Methodological Novelty

The novelty is best read against named prior art, one comparison at a time:

  • Versus completely-positive projection methods (Knee et al.). Standard pipelines repair an invalid estimate after the fact by projecting onto the positive cone. Here invalid states fall outside the model's expressive range: the Cholesky map lands in 𝒟(ℋ) by construction, eliminating the projection step entirely.
  • Versus Lindblad / Hamiltonian learning (Boulant et al.; Wang et al.). The framework retains structured identification while replacing unconstrained trajectory estimation with a hard density parameterization, so optimizer capacity stays inside the physical set throughout training.
  • Versus inverse PINNs (Raissi, Karniadakis; Wang et al.). The residual objective is shared, while the hypothesis class is narrowed to physically valid states and CPTP generators — the same structure-preserving principle behind Hamiltonian, Lagrangian, and symplectic networks, which constrain the model to improve learning.
  • Versus neural quantum states / neural tomography (Carleo & Troyer; Torlai et al.). Those parameterize wavefunctions or measurement distributions of states; this work parameterizes the generator of open-system dynamics, with a CPTP guarantee built in.
  • The "compiler" framing. A model is written as an intermediate representation — Hilbert dimension, Hamiltonian terms, jump operators, rates, observables, batching, differentiation mode — and lowered to dense, structured, sparse, or tensor-local kernels, aligning open-system identification with the design philosophy of modern accelerator and quantum software stacks.

6Theoretical Guarantees

Six exact results — not empirical trends — transcribed verbatim from the manuscript, each followed by a one-line reading of what it buys.

Theorem 1 — Exact density-matrix physicality. Let A:[0,T]→ℂ^{d×d} be continuous with A(t)≠0 for all t, and ρ_A(t)=A(t)A(t)^† / Tr(A(t)A(t)^†). Then ρ_A(t)∈𝒟(ℋ) for every t. If A is differentiable, ρ_A is differentiable, Hermitian, and Tr ρ̇_A(t)=0.
Every training step emits a legal state short of a floating-point failure — the mechanism behind the measured zero positivity-violation rate.
Theorem 2 — Universal approximation of continuous density trajectories. For any continuous ρ:[0,T]→𝒟(ℋ) and full-rank σ, and every ε>0, there exists a finite network A_φ with nonzero output such that sup_t ‖A_φA_φ^†/Tr(A_φA_φ^†) − ρ(t)‖_F < ε.
The hard constraint costs no expressive power: every continuous physical trajectory remains approximable inside the constrained hypothesis class.
Theorem 3 — GKSL preservation. With H_θ=H_θ^† and γ_k(ξ_k)=softplus(ξ_k)+γ_min, γ_min≥0, the generator is a GKSL generator and e^{t𝓛_Θ} is CPTP for all t≥0. The Kossakowski parameterization with C=BB^† is likewise a GKSL generator.
Because the learned generator is always a legal Lindbladian, it can be propagated to held-out initial states with an exact solver.
Theorem 4 — A posteriori residual certificate. Let ρ solve ρ̇=𝓛[ρ], ρ(0)=ρ_0 with 𝓛 generating a CPTP semigroup, and let ρ̂ be a differentiable Hermitian trace-one path with residual r(t)=ρ̂̇(t)−𝓛[ρ̂(t)]. Then $$\tfrac12\|\hat\rho(t)-\rho(t)\|_1\le\tfrac12\|\hat\rho(0)-\rho_0\|_1+\tfrac12\int_0^t\|r(s)\|_1\,ds.$$
The bound depends only on the learned path, so a small training residual is itself a computable guarantee on the trace distance to the true dynamics, computed without a reference trajectory.
Theorem 5 — Local identifiability. For a smooth GKSL generator with observation Jacobian J(Θ*) of full column rank and smallest singular value s_min>0, any estimate with ‖f(Θ̂)−f(Θ*)‖₂≤δ obeys ‖Θ̂−Θ*‖₂ ≤ 2δ/s_min on a suitable neighborhood.
Hard constraints cannot rescue uninformative observables: a near-singular sensitivity Gramian degrades the recovery bound — exactly the regime in which the qutrit rates are hard to pin down.
Theorem 6 — Dense versus structured residual complexity. For a d-dimensional system with m dense Lindblad operators: dense Liouvillian storage is O(d⁴), construction O(md⁴), and O(d⁴) per dense matvec; structured evaluation costs O(md³) arithmetic and O(md²) storage. Over N collocation states, dense is O(md⁴+Nd⁴) and structured O(Nmd³).
The per-evaluation ratio is Θ(d/m) and the storage ratio Θ(d²/m), so the structured route wins by a widening margin in both arithmetic and memory as d grows.

Two auxiliary results round out the theory: a Corollary extending physicality to low-rank-plus-isotropic variants, and a Proposition giving a Frobenius-norm analogue of the certificate via Grönwall's inequality.

8Empirical Validation

Five reproducible simulation studies anchor the claims. Each is repeated over five seeds — redrawing both the network initialization and the measurement-noise realization — and reported as mean ± 95% Student-t confidence interval. Four methods compete throughout: the hard-constrained CPTP-PINN (this work), a soft-penalty PINN (eigenvalue-negativity penalty), a fully unconstrained network, and a classical least-squares fit that is given the exact model structure.

9Significance and Impact

The central claim is that physicality belongs in the architecture as an invariant rather than in the loss as an afterthought, and the mathematics shows this invariant is nearly free: it costs nothing in expressivity (Theorem 2), keeps the learned generator CPTP (Theorem 3), supplies an a posteriori trace-norm certificate (Theorem 4), and admits structured compilation that sidesteps dense Liouvillian storage (Theorem 6). Device characterization ultimately requires a transferable open-system model rather than a fitted curve — a generator that propagates to held-out preparations at ≈0.97 fidelity. The constraint earns its keep exactly where practice demands it: physical trajectories guaranteed at every step, graceful degradation under sparse measurements, and a compiler path that holds storage and arithmetic below the squared density-vector dimension as d=2^n grows.

Limitations, stated in full.
  • Fast multi-level regimes remain the principal open problem. Direct reconstruction of fast qutrit (leakage) trajectories varies across seeds: an apparent single-seed Fourier-feature advantage dissolves under seed averaging (mean fidelity 0.77 ± 0.35 plain MLP against 0.52 ± 0.49 Fourier). Since a least-squares fit of the linear-in-rates dissipator to the exact trajectory recovers the rates to better than 1%, the bottleneck is derivative quality under noise rather than identifiability.
  • Simulation scope. The experiments are synthetic CPU simulations; they validate the implementation and the theory, leaving device performance to future hardware studies.
  • Markovian assumption. The framework presumes GKSL dynamics; non-Markovian systems require extended state spaces, memory kernels, or process tensors.
  • Identifiability is measurement-dependent. A rank-deficient sensitivity Gramian defeats any optimizer (Theorem 5).
  • Scale. This is a small, fully reproducible proof of concept (five seeds per experiment), distinct from a state-of-the-art benchmark sweep.

The clearest engineering path forward is a compiler that ingests a high-level noise model and emits fused, batched, differentiable kernels for accelerator execution. The scientific path forward follows naturally from the limitations above: interaction-picture residuals, derivative denoising, richer measurement designs, non-Markovian embeddings, and hardware-calibration datasets.

10Reproducibility

Every number in the manuscript is written directly to its figure and table file by the scripts in submission/code, with none entered by hand. The artifact is distributed as a pip-installable package, ctpcpinn (version 1.0.0, MIT, Python ≥ 3.10).

# 1. Obtain the source tree and install the package
cd submission/code
pip install .                 # runtime deps: numpy, scipy, torch, matplotlib, cycler

# 2. Regenerate every committed table and figure in place
python run_all.py             # or the console entry point:
ctpcpinn-reproduce
ctpcpinn-validate             # checks regenerated numbers against the manuscript

# 3. For bit-identical numbers, pin the exact environment
pip install ".[exact]"        # numpy 2.4.2, scipy 1.17.1, torch 2.10.0, matplotlib 3.10.8

Each training experiment runs over seeds {0,1,2,3,4} and reports mean ± 95% Student-t CI; the compiler benchmark reports the median over repeated timings. Configuration: 3000 Adam epochs at learning rate 10⁻³, 100 time points, noise standard deviation 0.02. The ctpcpinn/foundations.py module gives a from-scratch theory-to-code map, and run_one.py reproduces a single experiment. All simulations run on CPU; they are deliberately modest in scale and stand as a controlled proof of concept, separate from hardware data or state-of-the-art claims.
